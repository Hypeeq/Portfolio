Introduction

Returning to the Ontario Veterinary College (OVC) for another co-op term gave me an opportunity to build on an environment and role that I was already familiar with. Rather than spending the term learning how the organization and its systems operated from the beginning, I was able to use the knowledge and experience from my previous placement to take on more responsibility and work more independently.

As a Health Informatics Specialist, I supported technology used throughout the college's clinical and administrative environments. My work during this term included endpoint and identity management, infrastructure support, Azure Virtual Desktop (AVD), Azure DevOps, medical device deployment and configuration, RealWear device integration, and network-attached storage (NAS) infrastructure.

The most important outcome of this work term was not simply learning additional technologies. It was developing a better understanding of how individual technologies fit together within a larger IT environment. I became more comfortable taking ownership of tasks, investigating problems independently, documenting technical processes, and working with others to introduce and maintain technology. This term helped me move from primarily learning an enterprise environment to actively contributing to it.

Goals

This term focused on five connected areas of growth, from solving technical problems to building the confidence and communication skills needed to contribute independently.

01

Critical & Creative Thinking Problem Solving

My first goal was to strengthen my ability to independently analyze and resolve technical and workflow issues. I wanted to move beyond basic troubleshooting and develop a more structured approach to identifying root causes and understanding how different systems interact.

I achieved this goal through repeated exposure to infrastructure, endpoint, identity, and device-related problems. My previous experience with the environment allowed me to begin investigations with a better understanding of the systems involved rather than always requiring step-by-step direction. I worked with technologies such as Microsoft Intune, Entra ID, Autopilot, and AVD while investigating how devices, identities, access, and management systems interact.

One of the most valuable changes was learning to consider the larger system instead of treating every issue as an isolated problem. A device problem, for example, may involve configuration, identity, management, networking, or access rather than having a single obvious cause. This systems-level thinking made me more confident in investigating unfamiliar problems and deciding what information was needed before attempting a solution.

02

Communicating Oral Communication

My second goal was to improve how clearly and confidently I communicated technical information. In an IT environment, solving a technical problem is only part of the task; the solution also needs to be communicated to the people who depend on it.

Throughout the term, I participated more actively in discussions and became more comfortable explaining technical issues and progress. I learned to adjust the level of technical detail depending on the audience. Discussions with technical team members could focus on implementation and system behaviour, while communication with clinical or administrative users often needed to focus on what the technology does, how it should be used, and what they needed to know.

The work with RealWear devices was particularly useful for this goal. Technical documentation and user training were necessary parts of integrating the devices into the environment. This reinforced that good communication is not simply about explaining what was configured; it is about making sure another person can understand and successfully use what was implemented.

03

Literacy Technological Literacy

My third goal was to expand my understanding of enterprise IT through endpoint management, identity systems, workflow automation, and clinical technology.

I made significant progress in this area. I developed further experience with Microsoft Intune and Entra ID and gained hands-on exposure to Azure DevOps. I also worked with RealWear devices and learned more about how managed devices can be incorporated into an organization's existing infrastructure.

A major learning experience was configuring a NAS from the ground up. This involved working with storage, network shares, permissions, encryption, networking, backups, and remote access. This experience helped connect several areas of computing that are often learned separately. Storage is not simply about providing disk capacity; it also involves access control, security, networking, data protection, and recovery.

I also gained experience deploying and configuring medical machines for different clinical purposes, including X-ray, ultrasound, and MRI-related environments. Working with these systems demonstrated how enterprise IT concepts are applied to specialized technology and reinforced the importance of careful configuration and reliability.

Overall, this goal helped me develop a broader understanding of infrastructure. Instead of seeing technologies such as endpoint management, identity, storage, networking, and security as separate areas, I began to understand how they support one another.

04

Professional & Ethical Behaviour Teamwork

My fourth goal was to strengthen my teamwork by becoming more involved in projects, improving coordination, and contributing to smooth communication and transitions.

Returning to the team made this goal especially meaningful. Because I already understood many of the organization's systems and workflows, I could contribute without requiring the same level of initial guidance as during my previous placement. I became more involved with the team and took greater ownership of tasks.

Documentation was an important part of this. When introducing or configuring technology, the knowledge required to support it should not remain with one person. Creating technical documentation and supporting user training helped make the work easier for others to understand and maintain.

The RealWear integration was a good example of this approach. Configuring the devices so that they could be properly managed through the organization's device-management environment was only one part of the work. Documenting the setup and helping users understand the technology made the implementation more useful to the wider team.

This experience showed me that effective teamwork in IT is often about creating continuity. A technically successful solution becomes much more valuable when another person can understand it, support it, and continue working with it.

05

Personal Learning Goal Independence & Confidence

My final goal was to become more independent and confident by using the experience from my previous co-op term as a foundation.

I achieved this goal by taking greater ownership of tasks and requiring less detailed instruction. Returning to the same position meant that I could spend less time learning the basic environment and more time applying what I already knew. I was able to approach tasks with greater confidence, investigate problems independently, and become more involved in the team's work.

The NAS project was particularly valuable because it required me to understand and configure multiple aspects of an infrastructure system rather than focusing on a single predefined task. Similarly, working with RealWear devices and medical equipment required me to adapt existing knowledge to different technologies and requirements.

I also secured a student position within the department, which provided another indication of the trust and responsibility I had developed during my time with the team.

The biggest change I noticed was my relationship with unfamiliar problems. Earlier in my co-op experience, encountering a technology I had not used before could feel like a barrier. By the end of this term, I was more comfortable starting with what I knew, researching what I did not, asking appropriate questions, and working toward a solution independently.

Core Responsibilities

During this work term, I supported enterprise infrastructure and healthcare IT operations within the Ontario Veterinary College Health Informatics team. My responsibilities included troubleshooting technical issues, assisting with device management, supporting infrastructure-related tasks, and helping maintain reliable systems for staff and clinical users:

  • System Maintenance: I supported the ongoing stability and performance of enterprise healthcare IT systems by troubleshooting and resolving hardware and software issues, ensuring minimal disruption to clinical and administrative workflows.
  • Endpoint Management: I worked with enterprise device management platforms such as IGEL and Microsoft Intune to configure, maintain, and support endpoint environments, contributing to secure and standardized workstation deployments across the organization.
  • Ticket Management: I managed and resolved technical support requests through the ticketing system, applying structured troubleshooting approaches to deliver efficient and timely solutions for end users.
  • Networking & Infrastructure Support: I gained practical exposure to enterprise networking and infrastructure operations by assisting with connectivity issues and supporting underlying system functionality within a complex healthcare environment.
  • Access Control & Security: I supported user access management by ensuring appropriate permissions and role-based access controls were maintained, contributing to the protection of sensitive clinical and operational data.
  • Documentation: I maintained and contributed to technical documentation while communicating effectively with both technical and non-technical stakeholders, ensuring clarity in issue resolution, system updates, and operational procedures.

This role required technical troubleshooting, teamwork, communication, organization, and adaptability. While my coursework at the University of Guelph provided a strong foundation in programming and IT concepts, much of my knowledge related to enterprise infrastructure, networking, and endpoint management was developed through hands-on experience during the work term.

Conclusions

This work term represented a significant progression from my previous co-op experience at the Ontario Veterinary College. Returning to a familiar environment allowed me to spend less time learning the basics and more time contributing independently.

I expanded my technical knowledge through Microsoft Intune, Entra ID, Azure DevOps, AVD, RealWear devices, medical equipment, and NAS infrastructure. More importantly, these experiences changed how I think about IT systems. I became more aware of the relationships between devices, identities, networks, storage, security, users, and organizational processes.

The NAS work showed me the importance of understanding infrastructure as a complete system. The RealWear work demonstrated that successful technology adoption also requires documentation and user training. Deploying medical equipment reinforced the importance of careful configuration in environments where technology directly supports clinical workflows. My involvement with AVD provided exposure to larger infrastructure and environment-design considerations.

Personally, the biggest change was my increased independence. I became more comfortable taking ownership of tasks, investigating unfamiliar problems, communicating technical information, and contributing to the team without needing detailed direction.

Looking back at the goals I set at the beginning of the term, I believe I achieved each of them. I strengthened my problem-solving approach, improved my communication, expanded my technological literacy, became a more reliable team member, and developed greater professional independence.

Reflection

Returning to a familiar role at OVC accelerated my development from learning to contributing. With fewer orientation barriers, I focused on taking ownership of tasks, investigating issues end-to-end, and connecting individual technical problems to broader system behaviour.

Over the term I gained practical experience across endpoint and identity management, cloud-hosted desktop infrastructure, device deployment, and storage systems. Working with Microsoft Intune, Entra ID, Autopilot, AVD, Azure DevOps, RealWear devices, and a ground-up NAS configuration helped me understand how these elements interact in production environments and how changes in one area can affect others.

A key professional growth area was communication and documentation. I learned to tailor explanations for different audiences, produce documentation that enables handover, and design small training materials for clinical users. These practices made technical work more maintainable and increased team confidence in new deployments.

Personally, I became more independent and comfortable with unfamiliar technologies. The NAS and RealWear projects in particular required integrating knowledge across networking, storage, security, and device management, which strengthened my systems-level thinking. Securing a student position within the department also reflected the trust and responsibility I had earned.

Looking forward, this term clarified my interest in infrastructure, systems administration, and cloud-enabled endpoint management. I plan to continue deepening expertise in these areas while focusing on reliable deployments, clear documentation, and solutions that scale within clinical environments.

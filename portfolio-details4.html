Introduction

In my returning co-op term as a Health Informatics Specialist at the Ontario Veterinary College (OVC), University of Guelph, I further expanded my technical expertise and professional skills within an enterprise healthcare IT environment. Building on the experience gained during my previous placement, I worked on infrastructure support, endpoint management, troubleshooting, workflow optimization, and system administration while strengthening my communication, teamwork, and problem-solving abilities. During this term, I gained exposure to technologies such as IGEL, AVD, Corepoint, and Microsoft Intune, while also exploring networking principles, infrastructure management, and Python scripting. This experience reinforced my interest in enterprise IT and healthcare technology while continuing to develop my technical and professional confidence.

Learning Goals

For this work term, I focused on strengthening both my technical and professional skills by building on the experience gained during my previous Health Informatics placement:

  • Problem Solving: One of my primary goals was to further strengthen my problem-solving abilities by identifying technical, workflow, and infrastructure inefficiencies within existing systems. Throughout the term, I applied analytical thinking and prior experience to troubleshoot issues, evaluate potential solutions, and contribute improvements that supported more reliable and efficient workflows. Returning to a familiar environment also allowed me to approach challenges with greater confidence and independence.
  • Oral Communication: Another important goal was improving my oral communication skills by becoming more active and confident during meetings, troubleshooting discussions, and daily collaboration with team members. I focused on clearly explaining technical concepts and updates to both technical and non-technical users while adapting my communication style depending on the audience. This helped strengthen my confidence when discussing technical issues and collaborating on solutions.
  • Technological Literacy: A major goal for this term was expanding my technological literacy through hands-on exposure to enterprise infrastructure and management technologies. I gained experience working with IGEL, AVD, Corepoint, networking concepts, and Microsoft Intune while also exploring Python scripting and automation concepts. These experiences strengthened my understanding of infrastructure management, endpoint administration, and enterprise IT operations.
  • Teamwork: Since this was a returning work term, I also aimed to strengthen my teamwork and collaboration skills by contributing more actively within the Health Informatics team. I worked closely with technical staff, administrators, and users while participating in collaborative discussions and shared projects. Familiarity with the environment allowed me to communicate more effectively, support team continuity, and contribute more confidently toward shared goals.
  • Personal Learning Goal: Finally, I wanted to continue developing independence, adaptability, and self-confidence by taking greater ownership of responsibilities and applying lessons learned from my previous term. Managing support tasks, infrastructure-related work, and ongoing learning opportunities helped me improve my organization, time management, and ability to work independently in a fast-paced professional environment.

Overall, this work term allowed me to continue strengthening my technical knowledge, communication abilities, teamwork, and problem-solving skills while gaining deeper exposure to enterprise IT infrastructure and healthcare technology systems.

Core Responsibilities

During this work term, I supported enterprise infrastructure and healthcare IT operations within the Ontario Veterinary College Health Informatics team. My responsibilities included troubleshooting technical issues, assisting with device management, supporting infrastructure-related tasks, and helping maintain reliable systems for staff and clinical users:

  • System Maintenance: I supported the ongoing stability and performance of enterprise healthcare IT systems by troubleshooting and resolving hardware and software issues, ensuring minimal disruption to clinical and administrative workflows.
  • Endpoint Management: I worked with enterprise device management platforms such as IGEL and Microsoft Intune to configure, maintain, and support endpoint environments, contributing to secure and standardized workstation deployments across the organization.
  • Ticket Management: I managed and resolved technical support requests through the ticketing system, applying structured troubleshooting approaches to deliver efficient and timely solutions for end users.
  • Networking & Infrastructure Support: I gained practical exposure to enterprise networking and infrastructure operations by assisting with connectivity issues and supporting underlying system functionality within a complex healthcare environment.
  • Access Control & Security: I supported user access management by ensuring appropriate permissions and role-based access controls were maintained, contributing to the protection of sensitive clinical and operational data.
  • Documentation: I maintained and contributed to technical documentation while communicating effectively with both technical and non-technical stakeholders, ensuring clarity in issue resolution, system updates, and operational procedures.

This role required technical troubleshooting, teamwork, communication, organization, and adaptability. While my coursework at the University of Guelph provided a strong foundation in programming and IT concepts, much of my knowledge related to enterprise infrastructure, networking, and endpoint management was developed through hands-on experience during the work term.

Conclusions

This work term at the Ontario Veterinary College provided valuable experience working with enterprise infrastructure, endpoint management, and healthcare IT systems in a professional environment. By building on knowledge gained during my previous term, I was able to contribute more independently while developing stronger technical and analytical skills.

One of the most valuable aspects of this experience was gaining exposure to enterprise technologies such as IGEL, AVD, Corepoint, and Microsoft Intune. Working with these systems strengthened my technological literacy and gave me practical insight into how organizations manage devices, infrastructure, and user environments at scale.

Communication and collaboration also played a major role throughout the term. Working closely with technical staff and users taught me how important clear communication is when troubleshooting issues, supporting users, and contributing to collaborative projects. Returning to a familiar environment also helped me become more confident participating in discussions and taking initiative within the team.

Additionally, balancing support responsibilities, infrastructure-related tasks, and continuous learning opportunities improved my organization, adaptability, and time management skills. Exploring Python scripting and automation concepts further expanded my interest in improving workflow efficiency and enterprise system management.

Overall, this term strengthened all five of my learning goals-problem solving, teamwork, technological literacy, communication, and personal development-while reinforcing my interest in enterprise infrastructure, systems administration, and healthcare IT.
